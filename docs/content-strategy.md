# Content Strategy

## Professional Positioning

**Python Backend Developer | FastAPI, REST APIs & Automation**

Support remote employment and selected freelance work involving backend systems, APIs, automation, integrations, and internal tools. Demonstrate deliberate engineering, simplicity, clarity, and technical judgment through real work.

## Audiences and Their Questions

| Audience                    | Questions the narrative should answer                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Recruiters and hiring teams | What role fits? What has Sebastian built? How does he make and verify technical decisions? Where can we inspect evidence and contact him? |
| Freelance prospects         | Can he address this backend or workflow problem? What services are relevant? How does he approach delivery? How do we discuss a project?  |

Keep both paths clear without presenting unverified availability, experience, clients, or guarantees.

## Voice and Evidence Rules

The site must not read like a generic AI-generated developer portfolio. Use concise, specific, technically defensible language. Avoid “passionate developer,” “cutting-edge solutions,” “innovative digital experiences,” and unsupported expertise claims.

Before adding a common pattern, explain its narrative, technical, usability, or commercial purpose. Generic card grids, technology-logo walls, fake code or terminals, and skill percentages do not substitute for evidence. Visual anti-patterns are detailed in [design foundations](design-system.md).

Never fabricate employment, qualifications, skills, clients, metrics, or outcomes. Distinguish implemented behavior from intentions. When evidence is missing, flag the gap for confirmation rather than filling it with plausible copy.

## Project Storytelling

Use **Problem → Solution → Architecture/Decisions → Technology → Verification → Result/Learnings**.

- Problem: identify the real need and constraints.
- Solution: explain what was built and its actual scope.
- Architecture/Decisions: explain boundaries, tradeoffs, and reasons for choices.
- Technology: connect each relevant tool to its job.
- Verification: describe checks actually performed and their limits.
- Result/Learnings: report observed outcomes or concrete lessons; metrics require evidence.

Link repositories, demos, diagrams, or other supporting material only when available and verified. Do not imply production usage or a client relationship without confirmation.

## Job Radar as the Primary Case Study

Job Radar has two distinct roles: **primary technical case study** and **loose visual-family reference only**. The portfolio must not copy its layout or identity.

Build its story using the model above, confirming the problem, implemented features, architecture, technology, verification, and results from project evidence. Do not infer its stack from Sebastian's positioning or invent usage numbers, performance gains, users, or business results. Screenshots and final case-study copy are future work.
